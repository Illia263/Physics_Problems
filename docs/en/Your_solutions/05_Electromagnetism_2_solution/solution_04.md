**Concept:**
The magnetic torque ($\tau$) on a current-carrying loop is given by the product of the number of turns ($N$), the current ($I$), the area of the loop ($A$), the magnetic field strength ($B$), and the sine of the angle ($\theta$) between the area vector (normal to the plane of the loop) and the magnetic field.

**Given:**

- Current ($I$) = **2 A**
- Dimensions = 10 cm by 5 cm = **0.1 m by 0.05 m**
- Area ($A$) = $0.1 \times 0.05$ = **$0.005 \text{ m}^2$**
- Magnetic field ($B$) = **0.3 T**
- Angle ($\theta$): Since the magnetic field is applied parallel to the plane of the loop, it is perpendicular to the normal vector of the loop's area. Therefore, the angle $\theta$ between the area vector and the magnetic field is **$90^\circ$**.

**Formula:**
$$ \tau = N I A B \sin(\theta) $$
*(Assuming a single loop, $N = 1$)\*

**Calculation:**
$$ \tau = (1)(2)(0.005)(0.3)\sin(90^\circ) $$
$$ \tau = 2 \times 0.005 \times 0.3 \times 1 $$
$$ \tau = 0.01 \times 0.3 $$
$$ \tau = 0.003 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} $$

**Answer:**
The magnitude of the magnetic torque on the loop is **$0.003 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$**.
