**Concept:**
The magnetic field ($B$) generated by a long straight wire at a distance $r$ is given by $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$.
Because the wires carry currents in opposite directions, the right-hand rule dictates that their magnetic fields at the midpoint point in the same direction. Therefore, the total magnetic field is the sum of the individual fields.

**Given:**

- Current ($I_1 = I_2 = I$) = **5 A**
- Total distance between wires ($d$) = 10 cm = **0.1 m**
- Distance from each wire to midpoint ($r$) = $d / 2$ = 5 cm = **0.05 m**
- Permeability of free space ($\mu_0$) = **$4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$**

**Formulas:**
$$ B*{\text{single}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} $$
$$ B*{\text{total}} = B_1 + B_2 $$

**Calculation:**
$$ B*{\text{single}} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7})(5)}{2\pi(0.05)} $$
$$ B*{\text{single}} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{0.05} $$
$$ B\_{\text{single}} = 2 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T} $$

Since both fields point in the same direction at the midpoint:
$$ B\_{\text{total}} = 2 \times 10^{-5} + 2 \times 10^{-5} = 4 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T} $$

**Answer:**
The magnitude of the magnetic field is **$4 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$** (or $40 \text{ \mu T}$).
The direction is **perpendicular to the plane containing the wires** (either pointing directly into or directly out of the page, depending on the orientation of the currents).
