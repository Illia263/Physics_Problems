Here is the step-by-step breakdown for the tenth and final problem on your list! This one is a great wrap-up because it tests your understanding of how angles completely change the outcome of magnetic interactions.

### 10. Lorentz Force acting on a Wire

**Given:**
* Current in the wire: I = 10 A
* Length of the wire: L = 2.0 m
* Magnetic field strength: B = 0.5 T

**Goal:** Calculate the magnetic force (F) on the wire at three different angles: 90°, 45°, and 0°.

---

#### Step 1: The Force on a Wire Formula
Instead of a single particle flying through space, we now have a wire packed with billions of electrons all moving together (a current). The formula for the macroscopic magnetic force on a straight, current-carrying wire is:
$$F = I L B \sin(\theta)$$

Just like with the single particle, the angle ($\theta$) between the direction of the current and the direction of the magnetic field lines is the most important factor!

#### Step 2: Calculate for a) 90°
At 90°, the wire is perfectly perpendicular to the magnetic field. Since $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$, the wire takes the absolute maximum possible force.
$$F = I L B \sin(90^\circ)$$
$$F = (10) \cdot (2.0) \cdot (0.5) \cdot (1)$$
$$F = 10 \text{ N}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate for b) 45°
At 45°, the wire is cutting across the field lines at a diagonal. The sine function tells us exactly what fraction of the maximum force the wire will feel. Since $\sin(45^\circ) \approx 0.707$, the wire feels about 70.7% of the maximum force.
$$F = I L B \sin(45^\circ)$$
$$F = (10) \cdot (2.0) \cdot (0.5) \cdot (0.707)$$
$$F = 10 \cdot 0.707$$
$$F \approx 7.07 \text{ N}$$

#### Step 4: Calculate for c) 0°
At 0°, the wire is running exactly parallel to the magnetic field lines. It isn't "cutting" through them at all. Since $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$, the force drops completely to zero. 
$$F = I L B \sin(0^\circ)$$
$$F = (10) \cdot (2.0) \cdot (0.5) \cdot (0)$$
$$F = 0 \text{ N}$$

---

**Final Answer:**
a) At 90°, the force is **10 N**.
b) At 45°, the force is **7.07 N**.
c) At 0°, the force is **0 N**.
