### 5. Field Levitation

**Given:**
* Mass of a proton: $m_p = 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* Charge of a proton: $q = e = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Gravitational acceleration: $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$

**Goal:** Find the required electric field strength ($E$) to make the proton hover in mid-air.

---

#### Step 1: Understand the Physics of Levitation
For an object to "levitate," it means it is not accelerating up or falling down. According to Newton's laws, this means the net force acting on the proton must be exactly zero. 

Gravity is constantly pulling the proton downward toward the Earth. Therefore, to keep it suspended, the electric field must create an electric force that pushes the proton straight up with the exact same strength as gravity.

#### Step 2: Set up the Equation
We equate the magnitude of the upward electric force ($F_e$) to the downward gravitational force ($F_g$):
$$F_e = F_g$$

We know the formulas for both of these forces:
* Electric force in a uniform field: $F_e = qE$
* Gravitational force (weight): $F_g = mg$

Substitute these into our balance equation:
$$qE = mg$$

#### Step 3: Rearrange to solve for the Electric Field ($E$)
Divide both sides by the charge ($q$) to isolate $E$:
$$E = \frac{mg}{q}$$

#### Step 4: Calculate the final value
Now, carefully plug in the known constants:
$$E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27}) \cdot (9.80)}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}}$$
$$E = \frac{16.366 \times 10^{-27}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}}$$
$$E \approx 10.23 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N/C}$$

In proper scientific notation, we shift the decimal over by one:
$$E = 1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$$

---

**Final Answer:**
To levitate the proton, you need an electric field with a strength of **$1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$** directed upwards. *(Note: Because the proton is positively charged, it is pushed in the same direction as the electric field, so the field itself must be pointing up towards the sky).*
