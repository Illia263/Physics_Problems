### 8. Lorentz Force

**Given:**
* Charge of the particle: $q = 2.00 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Mass of the particle: $m = 4.00 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* Magnetic field strength: $B = 0.50 \text{ T}$
* Velocity of the particle: $v = 10^6 \text{ m/s}$
* Angle: Perpendicular ($\theta = 90^\circ$)

**Goal:** Find the magnitude of the Lorentz (magnetic) force ($F$) acting on the particle.

---

#### Step 1: The Lorentz Force Formula
When a charged particle moves through a magnetic field, it experiences a force. The magnitude of this force depends on the charge, the speed, the strength of the magnetic field, and the angle between the velocity and the field lines. 

The formula is:
$$F = qvB \sin(\theta)$$

*Note:* Did you notice that the problem gave us the mass ($m$) of the particle? We actually **don't need it** to find the force! Teachers often include extra information like this to test if you truly understand which variables belong in the equation. (You would only need the mass if the question asked for the particle's *acceleration* or the *radius* of its path).

#### Step 2: Set up the Equation
The problem states the particle enters the field "perpendicularly". In geometry, perpendicular means an angle of $90^\circ$. 
The sine of $90^\circ$ is exactly 1, meaning the particle is experiencing the absolute maximum possible magnetic force.

$$F = qvB \sin(90^\circ)$$
$$F = qvB \cdot (1)$$

#### Step 3: Calculate the Final Value
Plug in the remaining known values:
$$F = (2.00 \times 10^{-19}) \cdot (10^6) \cdot (0.50)$$

Multiply the standard numbers first: $2.00 \cdot 0.50 = 1.00$
Then combine the exponents: $10^{-19} \cdot 10^6 = 10^{-13}$

$$F = 1.00 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$$

---

**Final Answer:**
The magnitude of the Lorentz force acting on the particle is **$1.00 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$**.
