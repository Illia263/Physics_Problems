
### 1. Coulomb's Law

**Given:**
* Four corner charges: $q_1 = q_2 = q_3 = q_4 = +1.00 \text{ C}$
* Center charge: $q_0 = -2.00 \text{ C}$
* Side length of the square: $a = 1.00 \text{ m}$

**Explanation:**
The geometry of the square means the central charge $q_0$ is at the exact same distance ($r$) from all four corners. 

The magnitude of the electric force between the center charge and any one of the corner charges is given by Coulomb's Law:
$$F = k \frac{|q_0 q_i|}{r^2}$$

Because $q_0$ is negative and the corner charges are positive, the forces are attractive. The center charge is pulled directly toward each of the four corners with the exact same amount of force. 

Because of the symmetry:
* The force pulling $q_0$ toward the top-left corner is perfectly opposed by the force pulling it toward the bottom-right corner. 
* The force pulling $q_0$ toward the top-right corner is perfectly opposed by the force pulling it toward the bottom-left corner.

These opposing vectors cancel each other out completely, meaning the net force acting on the center charge is zero.

$$\vec{F}_{top-left} = -\vec{F}_{bottom-right}$$
$$\vec{F}_{top-right} = -\vec{F}_{bottom-left}$$
$$\vec{F}_{net} = \sum \vec{F}_i$$
$$F_{net} = 0.00 \text{ N}$$
