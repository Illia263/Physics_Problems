Here is the step-by-step calculus solution to find the current from the given charge function.

### **1. The Relationship Between Charge and Current**

Electric current, $I(t)$, is defined as the rate of flow of electric charge, $Q(t)$, over time. Mathematically, this means the current is the first derivative of the charge function with respect to time:


$$I(t) = \frac{dQ(t)}{dt}$$

### **2. Finding the Current Function**

You are given the charge function:


$$Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$$

To find the formula for current at any given time, take the derivative of $Q(t)$ with respect to $t$. The derivative of a constant (5) is 0, and we use the power rule for the $t^2$ term:


$$I(t) = \frac{d}{dt}(5t^2 + 5)$$

$$I(t) = 10t$$

### **3. Calculating Current at the Specific Time**

Now that we have the function for current, substitute the given time, $t = 3$ s, into the equation:


$$I(3) = 10(3)$$

$$I(3) = 30$$

### **Final Answer**

The current flowing through the wire at exactly 3 seconds is **30 A** (Amperes).
